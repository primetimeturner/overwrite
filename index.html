The show

        
In 2071 most of the planet spends its waking hours inside the Meridian, a simulated world big enough to hold every city ever built. Its most-watched broadcast is OVERWRITE: contestants are dropped into rebuildable arenas, crack open a Cache, steal the Data Core inside, and upload it before anyone else can take it from them.

        
Arenas are made of editable matter. Glass, signs, and sheet metal give way to gunfire. Concrete needs explosives. Steel frames hold structures up, and if you cut them, everything above comes down.

The arenas

        
Meridian Downtown at dusk: office towers with stairs to every floor. The Stackyard at night: trailer homes stacked on steel scaffolds. Climb the ladders, or cut the four corner columns to drop a whole stack. Harbor Nine by day: a warehouse with a glass roof, container rows, and a gantry crane you can bring down. Glitch Galleria at night: a two-storey mall under a glass roof, with escalators, shopfronts that shatter, and a hologram tower that drops if you cut its legs. Loop Line in the morning: a street market under two elevated train lines. Every span sits on a single pier, so one breach charge brings a span down, train and all. Test Site Seven at noon: a 1950s desert town built for a weapons test, with a diner, motel, and gas station on main street, test houses out in the flats, an observation bunker, a water tower, and a steel shot tower you can climb or topple.

        
Material guide

        
Glass and neon: 1 hit. Wall panels, crates, sheet metal, and ladders: a few rounds, and bullets penetrate at 60% damage. Concrete, floor slabs, and grating: explosives or the sledge. Steel frames: breach charges.
